This supplemental document provides detailed information on the data sources, collection procedures, processing steps, and computational implementation used to construct the first real-time dataset (RTD) of Peruvian GDP growth. It focuses on the methodological workflow, quality-control standards, and replication resources that underpin the empirical analysis.

In Peru, the National Institute of Statistics and Informatics (\href{https://www.inei.gob.pe/estadisticas/indice-tematico/economia/}{INEI}) produces and disseminates GDP statistics, while the Central Reserve Bank of Peru (\href{https://www.bcrp.gob.pe/publications/weekly-reports/weekly-report.html}{BCRP}) republishes these figures in its \href{https://www.bcrp.gob.pe/statistics/weekly-report-tables.html}{Weekly Report} (WR). Although INEI is the primary source, the BCRP provides a clearer and more consistent record of releases, reflecting legal obligations under article 84 of the Peruvian Constitution and articles 2 and 74 of the BCRP's Organic Law, which require the periodic publication of key national macroeconomic statistics. Our data collection strategy draws on both sources to construct the RTD of Peruvian GDP growth.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------
% 1. Revision Calendar
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\section{Revision Calendar}
\label{section:calendar}

A formal publication calendar governs the initial release of GDP growth figures, which are published with a two-month lag relative to the reference period across all frequencies. While this delay aligns with international practice, it underscores the need to account for data revisions when using GDP for real-time policy analysis. In contrast, no official revision calendar is maintained. This distinction is important to highlight: the timing of first releases is regulated and predictable, whereas subsequent revisions follow no explicit or public schedule.

For the construction of a RTD, however, a revision calendar is indispensable. Without a consistent schedule of updates, it becomes impossible to define vintages and track revisions systematically. A regular calendar ensures comparability across time, while irregular release dates complicate measurement, since the horizon between releases varies.

To address this limitation, we reconstructed an implicit revision calendar using the BCRP's WR. Each WR reproduces INEI's figures and specifies the date of the update. We then harmonized this information to a monthly frequency—consistent with the ``horizon'' definition in the main paper—based on two criteria:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \href{https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/normas-legales/2294897-316-2003-inei}{Chief Resolution No. 316-2003-INEI} mandates that sectoral offices update their data at least quarterly (e.g., March, June, September, December). These offices also produce monthly statistical information for the Monthly Index of National Production (IMPN), suggeting that revisions are made monthly.

    \item In the WR, revisions are effectively made on a month-to-month basis rather than week-to-week. Our reconstructed calendar confirms that the WR report updated GDP figures at least once per month.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Raw Data Sources}
\label{section:raw_data}

Once the monthly revision calendar was established, we compiled the last WR of each month. Each monthly WR defines a \emph{vintage} of GDP growth rates, which together form the basis of the RTD.

The WR republishes GDP growth rates by sector as released by INEI. These sectoral growth rates are reported year-on-year and presented in a well-organized table whose structure has changed little over the past three decades, although the table numbering has varied across issues. Two key tables were systematically extracted: one reporting monthly growth rates, and another reporting quarterly and annual growth rates

Historically, until the early 2000s, the WR was titled the ``Weekly Bulletin'', and no digital versions of either the Bulletin or the Report exist prior to 2013. Consequently, for the period spanning 2000–2012, the macroeconomic tables are only accessible in hardcover volumes available at the BCRP's \href{https://biblioteca.bcrp.gob.pe/discovery/search?vid=51BCDRDP_INST:51BCDRDP2}{Renzo
 Rossini Library}, requiring in-person consultation. These volumes cannot be removed from the library, but they may be photocopied or photographed. This process is not always straightforward, as the historical WRs are often bound in large, heavy volumes.

From 2013 to the present, however, WRs have been freely accessible on the BCRP's website in PDF format, which has greatly facilitated data collection. The data are also presented in spreadsheets and in the BCRP data database, although only in their revised form.

\subsection{Scanned Data (pre–2013)}

For the pre-2013 period, the WRs  were scanned into PDF format, producing non-structured hardcopies. Consequently, the extraction process was far from trivial and followed three main steps: (i) Computer-based extraction, (ii) manual correction and verification, and (iii) construction of parsed structured tables feeding into automated vintage-building routines implemented in the \pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.cleaners.old\_table\_cleaner} module.

The scanned sources posed significant challenges:
%
\begin{itemize}
    \item Warped or distorted text and misaligned table lines, often caused by hardcover bindings during scanning.
    \item Shadowing, opacity, or blur reducing legibility.
    \item Fragmented or incomplete tables.
    \item Highlighted entries interfering with optical recognition accuracy.
    \item Inconsistent font sizes across tables within the same report.
    \item Residual marks obscuring decimal points or digits.
    \item Ink and other physical spots disrupting recognition.
\end{itemize}

Figures~\ref{fig:wr_2_2007_table_1} and \ref{fig:wr_2_2007_table_2} show examples of scanned tables exhibiting some of these difficulties.

Given these limitations, data extraction was particularly demanding and did not provide definite results. We employed Optical Character Recognition (OCR) using the open-source \href{https://github.com/tesseract-ocr/tesseract}{\texttt{Tesseract}} engine in \texttt{Python}. Originally developed by Hewlett-Packard and now maintained by Google, \texttt{Tesseract} is widely used for digitizing printed material, including statistical reports.

\newpage
The OCR workflow consisted of:
%
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Converting pages into PNG format.
    \item Transforming visual text into machine-readable formats, with extensive pre-processing to improve accuracy:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Grayscale conversion and binarization to separate text from background.
            \item Noise removal (e.g., speckles, smudges) using median filtering or adaptive thresholding.
            \item Skew correction (deskewing) to align rotated text.
            \item Image scaling to ensure at least 300 DPI resolution.
            \item Contrast enhancement (e.g., via CLAHE) to make characters more prominent.
            \item Thinning and skeletonization to separate thick or touching characters.
            \item Border removal to avoid false detections.
        \end{itemize}
    \item Extracting tabular text into editable formats.
\end{enumerate}

Although OCR provided an initial transcription, frequent misclassifications occurred (e.g., reading \texttt{9.4} as \texttt{5.4}), making manual verification indispensable.

Once raw tables were extracted and converted to readable CSV format, we processed the data using the \pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.cleaners.old\_table\_cleaner} module and orchestrated the workflow through \pyfunction{old\_table\_1\_runner} and \pyfunction{old\_table\_2\_runner} functions. The pipeline involved:
%
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Data cleaning and validation using 70+ specialized transformation functions.
    \item Vintage-format conversion with target-event column structure.
    \item Concatenation across vintages to form complete RTD.
    \item Storage in both CSV and Parquet formats for accessibility.
\end{enumerate}

The combination of automated routines and manual oversight was essential for ensuring the reliability of the early vintages.



\subsection{Digital Data (post-2013)}

Since 2013, WRs have been published online as digital PDFs (semi-structured data), greatly facilitating automated extraction. We developed modular \texttt{Python} routines to handle heterogeneous table structures across reports. The primary extraction library was \texttt{tabula}, selected for its robustness in dealing with complex table layouts. All processing is implemented in the \pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.cleaners.new\_table\_cleaner} module, with workflow orchestration through \pyfunction{new\_table\_1\_runner} and \pyfunction{new\_table\_2\_runner} functions.

\newpage
Despite their advantages, digital sources also presented several recurring issues:
%
\begin{itemize}
    \item Encoded characters replacing numerical values (e.g., \texttt{(cid:101)(cid:115)}).
    \item Incomplete vertical lines separating columns, creating ambiguous parsing.
    \item Duplicate documents (e.g., \texttt{ns-21-2015} and \texttt{ns-45-2015}), disrupting scraping routines.
    \item Empty table cells misinterpreted as headers (e.g., \texttt{ns-25-2014}, \texttt{ns-14-2020}).
    \item Missing target-period labels, complicating the assignment of growth rates (e.g., \texttt{ns-28-2013}).
    \item Missing year labels, complicating the chronological alignment of data (e.g., \texttt{ns-28-2013}).
    \item Duplicated or misaligned headers generating spurious columns.
    \item Double or fragmented border lines, complicating table parsing.
\end{itemize}

The replication codes were designed to systematically address these problems through modular, configuration-driven architecture. All cleaning operations avoid hardcoding, ensuring adaptability to format changes and preserving consistency across vintages. The modular design consists of 70+ specialized functions organized into seven categories: text normalization (4 functions), general table cleaning (22 operations), column handling (14 functions), and table-specific transformations (13 functions each for Tables 1 and 2).

The automated extraction workflow, implemented through the \bashcmd{python scripts/update\_rtd.py} command, executes the following steps:

    \item Generating sentinel-adjusted datasets to mark invalid comparisons due to base-year changes.

    \item Converting vintage format to release format for econometric analysis (\pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.transformers.releases\_converter}).
    \item Storage of all dataset variants with comprehensive documentation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Real-Time Dataset (RTD)}
\label{section:rtd}
We emphasize the distinction between two key products:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Vintages dataset:} contains all successive records as originally extracted. This constitutes the real-time dataset (RTD).
    \item \textbf{Releases dataset:} derived from vintages, reorganized for revision analysis, and serving as the main input for our empirical tests.
\end{itemize}

The vintages dataset is the direct output of the extraction process and preserves the chronology of publications exactly as they appeared. By contrast, the releases dataset is a research-oriented transformation of the RTD, specifically designed to facilitate the study of revisions. Both formats are complementary: the vintages dataset ensures transparency and replicability, while the releases dataset provides the structure needed for econometric testing.

To illustrate the distinction between vintages and releases, we focus on a window covering the COVID-19 lockdown period, during which monthly GDP growth rates exhibit large negative values that are visually salient. Table~\ref{tab:vintages} presents the real-time dataset (RTD) in vintage format, where columns correspond to publication vintages (e.g., 2020m05, 2020m06, \ldots, 2021m05) and rows correspond to target periods (e.g., 2020m03, 2020m04, etc.). Each cell reports the value for a given target period as published in a specific vintage. To facilitate visual tracking across vintages, first releases are highlighted in blue, second releases in red, third releases in green, and fourth releases in yellow within each target-period row. For example, in the 2020m07 vintage column, the third estimate of March 2020 growth is collected (target period 2020m03 highlighted in green, $-16.30$), while the first estimate for May 2020 is collected (target period 2020m05 highlighted in blue, $-32.80$).

The releases dataset reorganizes the same underlying information by changing the unit of organization. Table~\ref{tab:releases} presents this alternative structure, where rows still correspond to target periods, but columns now group observations by release sequence rather than by vintage. Specifically, the column labeled First release ($y_t^1$) collects the first published estimate for each target period, Second release ($y_t^2$) collects the second estimate, and so on, up to the Last release ($y_t^H$). This mapping can be seen directly by tracking highlighted cells across tables. For instance, the blue cells corresponding to the first estimates of 2020m03 ($-16.30$), 2020m05 ($-32.80$), and 2020m07 ($-11.70$) appear in different vintage columns in Table~\ref{tab:vintages}, but they all appear together in the $y_t^1$ column of Table~\ref{tab:releases}. Likewise, the red cells (second releases) for the same target periods are grouped under $y_t^2$, the green cells under $y_t^3$, and the yellow cells under $y_t^4$.

This reorganization---standard in the real-time data literature---makes the revision process explicit and operational. Revisions are computed as differences between consecutive release columns (e.g., $y_t^2 - y_t^1$, $y_t^3 - y_t^2$). The Last release column ($y_t^H$) contains the most recent available estimate for each target period, after which no further economically meaningful revisions are observed. By making the flow of information updates transparent, the releases format provides a clean and interpretable structure for the rationality, efficiency, and benchmarking analyses conducted in the main paper.

\section{Computational Implementation and Replication}
\label{section:replication}

We provide a production-ready, open-source pipeline for constructing and updating the Peruvian GDP Real-Time Dataset. The codebase comprises over 4,000 lines of professionally documented Python code, organized into 14 specialized modules following software engineering best practices. All code is publicly available on GitHub (\url{https://github.com/JasonCruz18/peruvian_gdp_revisions}) under an MIT license, ensuring full transparency and reproducibility.

The pipeline architecture ensures complete reproducibility through configuration-driven execution, idempotent operations, and comprehensive error handling. A single command (\bashcmd{python scripts/update\_rtd.py}) executes the entire 6-step workflow from raw PDF download to final dataset generation, producing 10+ dataset variants suitable for econometric analysis. The modular design facilitates future extensions and adaptations to changes in data source formats while maintaining code maintainability and testability.

\subsection{Software Architecture}

The codebase follows a modular architecture with clear separation of concerns, organized into seven primary modules:

\paragraph{Configuration Module (\pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.config}):} Manages all pipeline settings through a 231-line YAML configuration file (\filepath{config/config.yaml}) and type-safe Python dataclasses. This design eliminates hardcoding entirely, enabling users to customize paths, scraping parameters, cleaning tolerances, and output formats without modifying source code. The configuration system uses Pydantic-style validation to ensure type safety and catch errors early.

\paragraph{Scrapers Module (\pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.scrapers}):} Implements automated web scraping from the BCRP website using Selenium WebDriver. The \pyfunction{pdf\_downloader} function handles authentication, rate limiting, retry logic with exponential backoff, and concurrent downloads while respecting server policies. The scraper automatically organizes PDFs by year and maintains download records for idempotent execution.

\paragraph{Processors Module (\pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.processors}):} Handles PDF processing tasks including keyword-based page extraction (\pyfunction{pdf\_input\_generator}), file organization (\pymodule{file\_organizer}), and metadata parsing (\pymodule{metadata}). These functions reduce raw 4-page Weekly Reports to 2-page inputs containing only GDP tables, significantly improving downstream processing efficiency.

\paragraph{Cleaners Module (\pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.cleaners}):} Contains 70+ specialized data cleaning functions organized into seven sub-modules:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \pymodule{text\_cleaners} (4 functions): Text normalization, whitespace handling, special character removal
    \item \pymodule{table\_cleaners} (22 functions): General DataFrame operations, noise removal, validation
    \item \pymodule{column\_handlers} (14 functions): Column renaming, type conversion, standardization
    \item \pymodule{table1\_cleaners} (13 functions): Monthly table-specific transformations
    \item \pymodule{table2\_cleaners} (13 functions): Quarterly/annual table-specific transformations
    \item \pymodule{old\_table\_cleaner} (1 class): Orchestrates cleaning for scanned CSV data (pre-2013)
    \item \pymodule{new\_table\_cleaner} (1 class): Orchestrates cleaning for digital PDF data (post-2013)
\end{itemize}

Each function is documented with type hints, docstrings, and example usage, ensuring transparency in data transformations.

\paragraph{Transformers Module (\pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.transformers}):} Implements data transformation and RTD construction through four specialized modules:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \pymodule{vintage\_preparator}: Reshapes cleaned tables into vintage format with target-period columns
    \item \pymodule{concatenator}: Merges vintages across years using union-based column alignment (372 lines)
    \item \pymodule{metadata\_handler}: Extracts revision metadata, applies base-year mappings, generates benchmark indicators (655 lines)
    \item \pymodule{releases\_converter}: Transforms vintage format to release format using NumPy-based vertical alignment (241 lines)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Orchestration Module (\pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.orchestration}):} Provides high-level workflow coordination through four runner functions (\pyfunction{old\_table\_1\_runner}, \pyfunction{old\_table\_2\_runner}, \pyfunction{new\_table\_1\_runner}, \pyfunction{new\_table\_2\_runner}) that orchestrate complete processing pipelines from raw data to structured vintages.

\paragraph{Utilities Module (\pymodule{peru\_gdp\_rtd.utils}):} Contains shared infrastructure including \pymodule{RecordManager} for idempotent operations and \pymodule{alerts} for user notifications.

\subsection{Pipeline Execution}

The pipeline supports both complete workflow execution and selective step execution through a command-line interface:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash, caption={Pipeline execution examples}]
# Execute complete pipeline (Steps 1-6)
python scripts/update_rtd.py

# Execute specific steps only
python scripts/update_rtd.py --steps 3,4,5,6  # Skip download/extraction

# Skip PDF download (useful for testing)
python scripts/update_rtd.py --skip-download

# Verbose output for debugging
python scripts/update_rtd.py --verbose

# Dry run (show execution plan without running)
python scripts/update_rtd.py --dry-run

# Use custom configuration file
python scripts/update_rtd.py --config path/to/custom_config.yaml
\end{lstlisting}

The six pipeline steps are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Download PDFs}: Automated scraping from BCRP website (2013--present)
    \item \textbf{Generate Inputs}: Extract GDP table pages from full PDFs
    \item \textbf{Clean \& Build RTD}: Process both OLD (CSV) and NEW (PDF) sources into vintage format
    \item \textbf{Concatenate}: Merge vintages across all years into unified RTD
    \item \textbf{Metadata}: Extract revision information, apply base-year mappings, generate benchmarks
    \item \textbf{Convert to Releases}: Transform vintage format to release format for analysis
\end{enumerate}

Each step maintains processing records to enable idempotent execution—rerunning the pipeline only processes new or modified data, not previously completed work.

\subsection{Data Cleaning Pipeline}

The data cleaning workflow applies 70+ specialized transformations to handle the formatting irregularities described in Section~\ref{section:raw_data}. Key cleaning operations include:

\paragraph{Text Normalization:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Unicode normalization (NFC form)
    \item Whitespace standardization (tabs → spaces, multiple spaces → single)
    \item Special character handling (accented characters, punctuation)
    \item Case normalization for sector names
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Table Structure Cleaning:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Empty row removal (fully NA or whitespace-only)
    \item Duplicate row detection and removal
    \item Header row identification and extraction
    \item Column alignment correction (handling irregular borders)
    \item Numeric column identification (distinguishing from text columns)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Numerical Data Cleaning:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Encoded character replacement (e.g., \texttt{(cid:101)} → valid numbers)
    \item Decimal separator normalization (comma → period)
    \item Thousands separator removal
    \item Type conversion with error handling (string → float)
    \item Missing value handling (empty strings, dashes, ``n.d.'' → NA)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Temporal Data Handling:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Missing year label inference (forward-filling from previous columns)
    \item Month abbreviation parsing (Spanish: ``ene'', ``feb'', etc.)
    \item Quarter identification (I, II, III, IV → 1, 2, 3, 4)
    \item Target period construction (year + month/quarter → ``2020m5'', ``2020q2'')
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Quality Assurance Features}

The pipeline implements several quality assurance mechanisms to ensure reliability and reproducibility:

\paragraph{Idempotent Operations:} The \pymodule{RecordManager} class tracks all processed files using text-based record files. Rerunning the pipeline automatically skips previously processed data, enabling incremental updates. Record files are human-readable and version-controlled for transparency.

\paragraph{Progress Tracking:} All long-running operations display \texttt{tqdm} progress bars with estimated completion times, colored output for status indicators, and summary statistics upon completion. This provides real-time feedback during execution and helps identify performance bottlenecks.

\paragraph{Error Resilience:} The pipeline implements graceful error handling with three-tier recovery:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Skip \& Continue}: Individual file failures don't halt entire workflow
    \item \textbf{Error Logging}: Failed operations are logged with full context for debugging
    \item \textbf{Summary Reports}: End-of-step summaries report success/failure counts
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Data Validation:} Built-in validation checks include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Schema validation (expected columns present, correct types)
    \item Range validation (growth rates within plausible bounds)
    \item Consistency checks (monotonicity of vintage dates)
    \item Completeness checks (missing value patterns)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Dual Persistence:} The pipeline supports both CSV (human-readable, universally compatible) and Parquet (compressed, type-preserving) formats. Output datasets include both formats by default, with CSV for inspection and Parquet for analysis.

\paragraph{Vintage Format Schema:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{industry} (string): Sector name (standardized)
    \item \texttt{vintage} (string): Publication month in ``YYYYmM'' format
    \item \texttt{tp\_YYYYmM} or \texttt{tp\_YYYYqN} or \texttt{tp\_YYYY} (float): Growth rate columns for each target period

\paragraph{Release Format Schema:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{target\_period} (string): Reference period in ``YYYYmM''/``YYYYqN''/``YYYY'' format
    \item \texttt{industry\_h} (float): $h$-th release for each industry (e.g., \texttt{agriculture\_1}, \texttt{agriculture\_2}, etc.)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Benchmark Datasets:}
Replace growth rates with binary indicators where 1.0 denotes a benchmark revision (simultaneous update of monthly and quarterly/annual tables) and 0.0 otherwise. These datasets enable isolation of methodological revisions from routine data updates.

\paragraph{Base-Year Adjusted Datasets:}
Apply sentinel value (-999999.0) to growth rates that become incomparable due to base-year changes. This marks invalid observations without removing them, preserving the complete revision history while flagging structural breaks.
